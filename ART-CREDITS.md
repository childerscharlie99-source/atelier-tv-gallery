The Commons API reports each artwork's full scan resolution; that is the `Original scan` figure. Most files were fetched as Wikimedia's allowed 3840 px renditions; three earlier full-resolution scans were used. For works whose scan is narrower than 3840 px, the 3840 px rendition (and then the 4K plate) is enlarged and marked upscaled; detail is limited by the original scan. The finished 3840×2160 plates are included in `app/assets/plates/american/`; the source scans are not part of this repository.

These thirty works ship in **Atelier 1.3.1**. Twenty-one are 4K crops or renditions of the source scan; nine are Lanczos-upscaled from narrower scans.

## Public-domain status

Every work was painted in the 19th century (1800–1899) by an American artist or an artist working in the United States. The underlying paintings are in the public domain in the United States as works published before 1930, and their artists died long enough ago to place them in the public domain in life-plus-70 jurisdictions.

The files used here are reproductions of two-dimensional public-domain works. Faithful photographic reproductions of such works do not create a new copyright in the United States (PD-Art). Each Commons file page carries the detailed public-domain tags and the source institution's provenance.

This is provenance information, not legal advice. Anyone republishing the images in another jurisdiction should confirm that jurisdiction's rules.

## Derivative-image method

The conversion was:

1. Open the downloaded source and convert to RGB.
2. Crop to a full-bleed 16:9 window. For sources wider than 16:9 the sides are cropped; for taller sources the height is cropped, using the per-work `crop_hint` when the peaks or waterfall would otherwise be lost.
3. Resize to exactly **3840×2160** with **Lanczos** only when the crop is smaller than 4K. No generative upscaling is used.
4. For upscaled plates only, apply a light unsharp mask (radius 1.2, 55%, threshold 3).
5. Save as **baseline (non-progressive) JPEG, quality 90, 4:4:4 chroma (subsampling 0), optimized**.

Cropping can remove parts of an original composition; the exact crop rectangle is recorded per work. Full Screen shows each plate full-bleed. Framed fills the opening of the wide white band with `object-fit: cover`; because that opening is slightly wider than 16:9, a few percent of the top and bottom is trimmed rather than showing white side bars. Idle keeps the frame while clearing every other control. The 3840×2160 plate is scaled to the TV's 1920×1080 web-app surface, which the 4K panel then displays.

## Frame finish

The single visual frame finish is **Modern White**, the white bezel Samsung uses on The Frame TV. It is shown and named in Samsung's UK buying guide, [Introducing The Frame TV](https://www.samsung.com/uk/tvs/tv-buying-guide/introducing-the-frame-tv/); the article also lists Teak and Brown, but Atelier keeps only the white finish.

- **Modern White** — based on the Modern White add-on bezel (e.g. `VG-SCFH65WTBXY` / `VG-SCFF65WT`), a satin off-white bezel. Atelier draws it as a single **72 px** CSS border band with a fine outer rim and a soft dark mouth shadow. Because it is one continuous border rather than four rails, it has no mitre lines or corner triangles, and the painting fills the opening edge to edge with no white mount.

Sources (checked 2026-10-24):

- [Samsung UK — Introducing The Frame TV](https://www.samsung.com/uk/tvs/tv-buying-guide/introducing-the-frame-tv/) — names the Modern White, Teak, and Brown bezels.
- [Samsung Australia — 65″ Modern White bezel](https://www.samsung.com/au/tv-accessories/customizable-frame-vg-scfh-vg-scfh65wtbxy/)
- [PC Richard — 65″ Modern White bezel, compatibility listing](https://www.pcrichard.com/samsung-the-frame-65-customizable-bezel-modern-white-compatible-with-samsung-2021-2025-frame-tvs-and-2025-frame-pro-tvs/VG-SCFF65WT.html)

No Samsung photograph, logo, product image, or licensed texture is bundled. The rails and preview are generated from CSS gradients. Their color is an approximation, will vary with the panel's picture settings, and is not presented as an exact match. The television's own stock black/charcoal bezel remains underneath; Atelier cannot change physical hardware. Samsung has not reviewed, sponsored, or endorsed Atelier.
